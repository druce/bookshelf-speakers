# Stereophile: Dynaudio Special Forty review (John Atkinson, 2018-08-30)

## Summary
Atkinson (pages 1-2; second page in raw/reviews-critic/stereophile-review-p2-dynaudio-special-forty.md) praised the weight and cleanliness of the bass, stable imaging, clean treble and voices, but also heard slightly emphasized 200-600 Hz and a midrange congestion he traced to resonant sidewall modes. He judged the issue an "Achilles' heel" of modest weight, noting that nine other reviews he read did not mention it.

## Key claims
- Pink noise: smooth and even with slight upper-midrange emphasis; narrow, stable central image ("inherently accurate" stereo imaging).
- Bass: powerful down to the 50 Hz band, 40 Hz warble lower in level, 25 and 20 Hz inaudible at normal levels; no audible doubling above 63 Hz; ports left open.
- Top octave a touch mellow; high frequencies clean and fatigue-free.
- Cabinet: sidewall resonances audible with a stethoscope; stand-interface materials changed the effect (upturned cones reduced congestion).
- Verdict: does a lot exceptionally well for its size; price $2,999/pair at review. He called it limited-edition (Dynaudio's current page lists it as current; see speaker page Conflicts).

## Method notes
Celestion 24" stands, Lamm M1.2 Reference monoblocks, Roon music. Measurements: [[stereophile-dynaudio-special-forty]].

## Reliability notes
Single reviewer and sample. The cabinet observation is corroborated by his own measurements, and he warns against over-weighting it.
