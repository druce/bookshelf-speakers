# Audioholics: Dayton Audio OPAL1 review (2024-08-30)

## Summary
James Larson praises the OPAL1's sub-30 Hz in-room bass for its size, build quality and imaging, and flags the very low sensitivity (measured 76 dB) and an erratic anechoic-style measured response that he argues will be smoother in room. Performance 4.5/5, value 5/5 at $800/pair MSRP.

## Key claims (Tier D, attributed)
- Larson heard terrific centre imaging and a wide stage on vocal and string recordings.
- Bass sounded subwoofer-like and held composure; he saw woofer movement but judged it had more in reserve (14 mm Xmax).
- Cons listed: needs lots of amplification for more than modest loudness; erratic measured response.
- Considers nominal 8 Ω "a stretch" but acceptable: tweeter band sits under 5 Ω, bottoming near 4 Ω at 5 kHz; woofer region stays at or above 8 Ω.

## Method notes
Free-air measurement at 7.5 ft height, 1 m, gated at 11 ms: resolution lost below 250 Hz and accuracy lost below 110 Hz; 1/24-octave smoothing. Ground-plane low-frequency measurements also shown. Not a Klippel full-sphere set, so treated as Tier B.

## Reliability notes
Single review sample, listening in a 24x13 ft room with Marantz 7705 and Monolith 5x200 amplification. Reviewer argues anechoic curve overstates irregularity versus in-room.
