# Stereophile: PS Audio Aspen FR5 (2024-11-21)

## Summary
Kalman Rubinson's review is lengthy and positive; John Atkinson's quasi-anechoic measurements (MLSSA) show a relatively even response, a low sensitivity (82.6 dB), an easy impedance magnitude but EPDR dipping to about 2.6 Ω, and a vertical suck-out around the crossover off axis. Review text is in raw/reviews-critic/stereophile-ps-audio-aspen-fr5.md, -p2.md and -specs.md.

## Key claims (Tier D, Kalman Rubinson)
- Initial maker-recommended setup (near front wall, toe-in to 3' behind listener) gave narrow stage and weak presence; moving to about 3' from front wall with minimal toe-in made it "open up".
- Bass full for a 6.5" pair but less impact and definition in the lowest octave; at high levels output coarsens well up into the midrange. Recommends adding subwoofer(s).
- Treble detailed without brightness (grilles off helped); upper midrange slightly depressed next to KEF Blade Two and LS60, which suited forward recordings.
- Wide, deep soundstage; named equals in clarity to Bowers & Wilkins 705 S3 without that speaker's treble lift.
- Conclusion: hard to criticize; "generous bass for their size". Extra raw files: raw/reviews-critic/stereophile-ps-audio-aspen-fr5.md, -p2.md, -specs.md.

## Method notes
DRA Labs MLSSA, DPA 4006 mic, farfield at 50" on tweeter axis, 30-degree horizontal window average, grille off. Low-frequency data from nearfield. Impedance by Dayton DATS V2. No distortion or compression results. No listening-window or spinorama figures published.

## Reliability notes
Single sample (serial FR5-A-4E0303/4). Review sample supplied by the maker. Quasi-anechoic, so Tier B below about 300 Hz.
