# On-wall speakers

A separate category in this wiki (Claude.md §2). It covers passive speakers designed by their maker to hang flat on a wall as main left/right (or LCR) speakers. They are ranked only against each other.

## Why they are different
- **Boundary gain:** a source flush against a wall radiates into half-space below the frequency where the cabinet and baffle stop being directional, roughly a few hundred hertz. That adds up to about +6 dB of bass compared with free space. A wall-voiced design builds in a matching bass cut, or relies on the wall to fill the baffle-step loss. On a stand it sounds thin; on the wall it sounds balanced. An ordinary bookshelf speaker hung on a wall does the opposite and gets heavier.
- **No wall-reflection notch:** the speaker sits on the boundary, so the floor-to-ceiling and front-wall cancellation dip that standmounts suffer (the "speaker boundary interference response") largely disappears. This is a real advantage.
- **No toe-in, early side reflections:** directivity matters more. Wide, smooth horizontal dispersion, angled baffles or waveguides help.
- **Shallow cabinets:** typically 75–150 mm deep, so small enclosure volume. Expect high bass roll-off, limited low-frequency output, and a subwoofer as standard.
- **Port:** sealed or front-ported. A rear port has nowhere to go.

## Reading measurements of on-walls
- A free-field spinorama (ASR or Erin's Klippel scan, NRC chamber) shows the speaker without its wall. Wall-voiced designs look bass-light below roughly 300–500 Hz, and their free-field f3 (e.g. 193 Hz for the now-discontinued KEF T101 measured by ASR; ~125–151 Hz for [Paradigm Millenia LP 2](../speakers/paradigm-millenia-lp-2.md)) is not comparable with bookshelf f3. The bookshelf labels in Claude.md §7 (excellent/good/modest) are not applied.
- The right bass reference is a half-space or large-baffle measurement. Few reviewers make one, and pages state which kind each source used.
- Listening-window flatness above ~500 Hz, directivity, distortion and impedance compare normally. The [preference-score](preference-score.md) with sub is the more meaningful of the two scores for on-walls.
- `wall_voiced` on each page records whether the maker states boundary compensation (true/false/unknown).
