# Ranked by price band

Current, in-scope speakers with a spinorama-derived Olive preference score, sorted by **score with an ideal subwoofer** within each price band. Small bookshelf speakers are compared fairly on that score because bass extension dominates the no-sub score ([[preference-score]]). Per Claude.md §7, differences under 0.5 are not meaningful. Scores from Tier B hobbyist rigs are marked (B) and are less comparable. **Group A** = within 0.5 of the band leader; **Group B** = within 1.0. Rank inside a group is noise. The score ignores distortion, output and amplifier load, so the table also carries LW deviation ([[listening-window]]), f3 ([[bass-extension-vs-room-gain]]), sensitivity, measured minimum impedance with its flag ([[impedance-and-epdr]]) and THD at 96 dB. Labels follow Claude.md §7. Generated by `scripts/rank.py` from `data/speakers.csv`.

**Cross-band observations (computed from the table, Tier A only):**
- [[ascend-acoustics-cbm-170-se2]] ($468, pref w/sub 8.0) is within 0.5 of **every** band leader above it: [[ascend-acoustics-sierra-1-v2]] 8.4, [[revel-m105]] 8.2, [[march-audio-sointuva-awg]] 8.45. With a subwoofer, spending more than about $500 a pair buys bass extension, output, load and finish rather than predicted tonal preference, at least as far as the Olive model can see.
- Of the 21 Group A/B models, only 3 have a measured minimum that clears the IEC floor ([[elac-debut-2-0-b5-2]], [[ascend-acoustics-sierra-1-v2]], [[mofi-sourcepoint-8]]). Three have no measured minimum ([[ascend-acoustics-sierra-lx]], [[buchardt-audio-e50]], [[q-acoustics-3030i]]), and the other 15 are flagged. In practice, the best-measuring passive bookshelf speakers are mostly 4 Ω-class loads. See [[impedance-honesty]].
- f3 values of 93–103 Hz for [[revel-m126be]] and [[q-acoustics-5020]] come from a shallow LW shelf crossing −3 dB well above the real roll-off (f6 ≈ 50 Hz for the M126Be); see each page's Conflicts and [[bass-extension-vs-room-gain]].
- `max_spl_db` is **not** used for ranking: ASR draft compression charts, EAC's 102 dB test ceiling and stated limits are not comparable.
