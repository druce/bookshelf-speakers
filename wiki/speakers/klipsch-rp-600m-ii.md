## Summary
The Reference Premiere RP-600M II is a rear-ported 6.5" standmount with a 1" titanium dome on a 90x90° Tractrix horn, claimed at 94.5 dB and "8 Ω compatible". Erin's Klippel data show a fairly good listening window (±1.9 dB) with a treble lift from about 8 kHz, modest bass (f3 68 Hz) and real sensitivity near 85 dB, about 9 dB under the claim. Impedance falls to 3.5 Ω, so it is effectively a 4 Ω load despite the 8 Ω label. Suits listeners wanting lively, wide-dispersion sound at about $650-700 per pair who accept a bright-leaning balance; an amp stable into 4 Ω is advisable.

## Directivity
Horizontal -6 dB beamwidth about 94°, vertical about 78°; Erin calls horizontal directivity good (about ±50-60°) and vertical weaker. DI has a minor discontinuity (ERDI residual 1.8 dB); SPDI rises from 4.7 dB at 1 kHz to 10.6 dB at 10 kHz. Early-reflection dip near 1.5 kHz is 0.8 dB, well under 3 dB. On-axis minus listening window above 4 kHz is +1.3 dB, so the on-axis treble is hotter than the window: Erin suggests turning the speakers about 20° outward (aim past the listener) or using the grille. See [directivity-index](../concepts/directivity-index.md), [early-reflections](../concepts/early-reflections.md).

## Output & distortion
At 86 dB @ 1 m THD peaks near 0.7% around 100-150 Hz and is lower above; at 96 dB it reaches about 2.0% near 100 Hz and stays under about 1% above 1 kHz (graph-read). Compression is small: up to about 1 dB at 102 dB @ 1 m, mainly 100-150 Hz. No formal max SPL is stated. Good for medium rooms at normal-to-loud levels; the limit is bass depth, not compression. See [compression-and-max-spl](../concepts/compression-and-max-spl.md).

## Amplifier load
Maker says "8 Ω compatible", but Erin measured 3.5 Ω minimum above 80 Hz (flag `effectively-4ohm`) with EPDR down to 1.8 Ω, and phase up to about 60° near 1.3 kHz and -50° near 100 Hz. Peaks reach 26 Ω near 2.4 kHz, which suits tube amps tonally. Use an amp stable into 4 Ω; the real sensitivity (85 dB) means it is not as easy to drive as the 94.5 dB claim suggests. See [impedance-and-epdr](../concepts/impedance-and-epdr.md).

## Critic reviews
- [stereophile-klipsch-rp-600m-ii](../sources/stereophile-klipsch-rp-600m-ii.md) (Stereophile, Herb Reichert): more refined than the original, even bass for a ported standmount, works with a 3.5 W tube amp.
- [hifitrends-klipsch-rp-600m-ii](../sources/hifitrends-klipsch-rp-600m-ii.md) (HiFi Trends, 8.7/10): detailed with strong imaging and bass control, but brighter on solid-state; needs matching.
- [stereonet-klipsch-rp-600m-ii](../sources/stereonet-klipsch-rp-600m-ii.md) (StereoNET): big, fun and dynamic but not neutral; better for rock and pop than classical.
- [eac-klipsch-rp-600m-ii](../sources/eac-klipsch-rp-600m-ii.md) (Erin, Tier D notes): one of the more neutral Klipsch speakers; treble boost from about 8 kHz.

## User reviews
Crutchfield ([crutchfield-klipsch-rp-600m-ii](../sources/crutchfield-klipsch-rp-600m-ii.md); 217 reviews, 182 five-star, hand-tallied): treble divides reviewers, 11 of 217 calling it bright or fatiguing and 13 of 217 explicitly not; about 29 of 217 praise bass for the size (5 of 217 find it light); 7 of 217 call it easy to drive; at least 6 of 217 say it plays loud cleanly; 7 of 217 prefer it to the Mk I. The bright reports agree with Erin's treble lift from about 8 kHz. Earlier thin reports ([users-klipsch-rp-600m-ii](../sources/users-klipsch-rp-600m-ii.md)) also mention fatigue at volume.

QC/reliability: no driver or electrical failures in 217 reviews; 1 delivery-damage case resolved.

## Conflicts
- Sensitivity: claimed 94.5 dB (half-space) vs 85.4 dB Klippel (Erin states 86 dB); about 9 dB gap, more than half-space gain explains. Measurement takes precedence.
- Impedance: labelled "8 Ω compatible" vs measured 3.5 Ω minimum (effectively 4 Ω).
- Bass: claimed 44 Hz (-3 dB) vs measured f3 68 Hz anechoic.
- Reviewers split on treble: Erin and HiFi Trends hear a lift or brightness, Stereophile finds the top smoother than the original.
- Drive difficulty: 7 of 217 Crutchfield reviewers call it efficient or easy to drive (one estimates real sensitivity near 90 dB), versus measured 85.4 dB and 3.5 Ω minimum, about 9 dB under the 94.5 dB claim.
