## Summary
The SVS Ultra Evolution Bookshelf is a 6 ohm rated, rear-ported two-way standmount with a 1" diamond-coated aluminium dome, a 6.5" glass-fibre woofer and a sloped, time-aligned cabinet, at about $1,200 per pair. Its only Tier-B measurement (Stereophile, Temme) shows sensitivity matching the 87 dB claim, a response within +/-3 dB from 40 Hz, low distortion at 90 dB, and an impedance minimum of 3.7 ohm. There is no spinorama; directivity is described only as broad with lobing at 3-7 kHz. Critics agree it has unusually good bass for its size. Suits listeners wanting scale from a small speaker who can feed a 4 ohm-capable amplifier.

## Directivity
No beamwidth or DI figures. Stereophile's horizontal polars are mostly omnidirectional to 15 kHz with off-axis lobing at 3-7 kHz; vertical is also broadly wide with rough spots at 3-7 kHz. Temme advises against shelf placement near a rear wall because of early reflections. Group delay is low thanks to the sloped baffle. See [directivity-index](../concepts/directivity-index.md), [early-reflections](../concepts/early-reflections.md).

## Output & distortion
Harmonic distortion at 90 dB is below 0.5% above 100 Hz (Tier B), excellent. No compression test. Critic Tom Fine found it clean at loud levels but unable to fully load a room with bass at low listening levels, suggesting a sub; Pragmatic Audio and Audioholics were more positive about bass weight. See [compression-and-max-spl](../concepts/compression-and-max-spl.md).

## Amplifier load
Rated 6 ohm; measured minimum 3.7 ohm near 200 Hz, below 4 ohm, flagged `effectively-4ohm` (also below the 4.8 ohm IEC floor for 6 ohm). Stereophile saw a small phase angle at the minimum and called it an easy load; Audioholics drove it happily with a 25 W integrated. EPDR not computed. Maker suggests 20-150 W. See [impedance-and-epdr](../concepts/impedance-and-epdr.md).

## Critic reviews
- [stereophile-review-svs-ultra-evolution-bookshelf](../sources/stereophile-review-svs-ultra-evolution-bookshelf.md) (Stereophile, Tom Fine): better extension and a brighter top end than KEF LS50 Meta; shines with the subwoofer.
- [audioholics-svs-ultra-evolution-bookshelf](../sources/audioholics-svs-ultra-evolution-bookshelf.md) (Audioholics, 5/5 performance and value): weightier bass than any 6.5" two-way near the price, warm-leaning balance, smooth treble.
- [pragmaticaudio-svs-ultra-evolution-bookshelf](../sources/pragmaticaudio-svs-ultra-evolution-bookshelf.md) (Pragmatic Audio): very strong bass extension in room; imaging a touch behind a KEF LS50 Meta.

## Conflicts
- Nominal impedance: maker and Stereophile say 6 ohm; one reseller SKU lists 8 ohm. Maker value used.
- Bass: Stereophile's measurement and Fine's listening show real extension to about 40 Hz but limited room-filling output at low levels; Pragmatic Audio claims in-room bass toward 30 Hz (room gain, not comparable).
- Treble: Fine found the top end brighter than a KEF LS50 Meta, Green found it smooth and non-fatiguing (different reference speakers and rooms).
