## Summary
The Dynaudio Special Forty is a 6 Ω, two-way, rear-ported standmount with a 28 mm Esotar Forty soft dome, a 17 cm MSP woofer and a first-order 2 kHz crossover, launched in 2017 for the brand's 40th anniversary. Klippel data show a listening window of +/-2.2 dB, f3 of about 52 Hz and a low 83 dB sensitivity (claimed 86 dB). Its distinctive feature is a cabinet and port resonance in the 500-700 Hz region visible in the NRC, EAC and Stereophile data and audible to Stereophile. It suits small-to-medium rooms and listeners who value Dynaudio's bass weight and voice reproduction, with a competent amplifier. Price and availability are uncertain (see Conflicts).

## Directivity
Horizontal -6 dB beamwidth about 146°, vertical about 93°. DI is a minor discontinuity (ERDI residual 1.9 dB); the early-reflection dip near 1.5 kHz is 3.4 dB, over the 3 dB flag. SPDI rises from 5.7 dB at 1 kHz to 8.5 dB at 10 kHz. Stereophile reports controlled lateral dispersion up to 7 kHz and, because of the first-order crossover, a vertical suckout above the tweeter axis, so the flattest balance is on or just below the tweeter axis. On-axis minus listening window above 4 kHz is +0.7 dB, so either aim works; aim the tweeter at ear height or slightly above. See [directivity-index](../concepts/directivity-index.md), [early-reflections](../concepts/early-reflections.md).

## Output & distortion
EAC's THD plots show about 0.8% worst case at 86 dB and about 2.0% at 96 dB (graph-read), with a distortion peak near 500 Hz and about 2% in the 80-100 Hz region at 96 dB. The compression test reached 102 dB @ 1 m with about 1.1 dB loss near 130 Hz and about 1.5 dB at 40 Hz, so no 3 dB limit was found, and no max-SPL figure is stated. NRC's THD+N at 90 dB peaks near 2% around 100 Hz. SoundStage! found it clean at high levels in a large room but not able to match big floorstanders. Fine for small-to-medium rooms at moderate-to-loud levels. See [compression-and-max-spl](../concepts/compression-and-max-spl.md).

## Amplifier load
6 Ω nominal. Minimum 4.9 Ω around 180 Hz, just above the IEC floor of 4.8 Ω, so no flag. EPDR dips to 2.5 Ω near 120 Hz, which is demanding for the nominal rating; phase reaches about -54° at 94 Hz where impedance is high. With 83-84 dB sensitivity, use an amplifier comfortable into 4 Ω with moderate power. See [impedance-and-epdr](../concepts/impedance-and-epdr.md).

## Critic reviews
- [soundstage-dynaudio-special-forty](../sources/soundstage-dynaudio-special-forty.md) (SoundStage!, Reviewers' Choice): very uncolored after run-in, clean tight bass, wide soundstage; no 20-40 Hz.
- [stereophile-review-dynaudio-special-forty](../sources/stereophile-review-dynaudio-special-forty.md) (Stereophile): powerful clean bass, stable imaging, slightly mellow top octave; midrange congestion from cabinet resonances.
- [tas-dynaudio-special-forty](../sources/tas-dynaudio-special-forty.md) (The Absolute Sound, very highly recommended): full-bodied, deep bass for its size, big dynamics.

## User reviews
Only 3 relevant forum comments, so anecdotal ([users-dynaudio-special-forty](../sources/users-dynaudio-special-forty.md)): all 3 unimpressed relative to price, none confirmed owners. No QC problems reported.

## Conflicts
- Sensitivity: 83.2 dB (EAC, computed), 83.6 (NRC), 84.4 (Stereophile) vs 86 claimed; range 1.2 dB across measurers, and 1.6-2.8 dB below the claim.
- Minimum impedance: 4.9 Ω (EAC and Stereophile) vs about 4.7 Ω graph-read (NRC); within 0.5 Ω.
- Cabinet/port resonance: Stereophile measured a strong 512 Hz sidewall mode and 700 Hz port spike and heard congestion; SoundStage! and TAS reported none. NRC's listening-window curve shows a narrow peak near 550 Hz and EAC's distortion and compression plots show features near 500 Hz, which fits Stereophile's measurement; audibility is not resolved.
- Status: Dynaudio's page lists it Current in the Editions series and SoundStage! said it is not limited, but Stereophile called it limited-edition and a TMR Audio listing is sold out.
- Price: retailers show $3,599 (Audio Vision SF) vs $2,999 at launch; MSRP $3,500 on a TMR listing; stock unclear.
