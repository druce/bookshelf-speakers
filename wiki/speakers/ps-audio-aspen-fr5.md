## Summary
The PS Audio Aspen FR5 is a 6 Ω, two-way, 6.5" standmount with a 2.5" planar-magnetic tweeter and a rear 6x9" passive radiator, sold direct for $3,499 per pair. Klippel data show a listening window of ±2.7 dB with a wide, fairly even directivity and f6 of 36 Hz (f3 51 Hz). Sensitivity is low (about 81-83 dB) and the EPDR dips to about 2.5 Ω, so it wants a capable amplifier even though the impedance magnitude stays above 5.6 Ω. Suits listeners wanting unusually deep bass from a small box and a smooth top end, with a subwoofer still helpful at high levels.

## Directivity
Horizontal -6 dB beamwidth about 149°, vertical about 95° ([eac-ps-audio-aspen-fr5](../sources/eac-ps-audio-aspen-fr5.md)). DI is classed minor-discontinuity (ERDI residual 1.9 dB; the 0-degree file says major-discontinuity at 2.1 dB). SPDI rises from 4.7 dB at 1 kHz to 7.6 dB at 10 kHz. The early-reflection dip near 1.5 kHz is 3.3 dB, just over the 3 dB flag. Stereophile found a vertical suck-out in the crossover region more than 10° below and 20° above the tweeter axis, so stand height matters. On-axis minus listening window above 4 kHz is +0.4 dB, so either aim works; Stereophile preferred minimal toe-in, while HFN found 15° toe-in tightens the response. See [directivity-index](../concepts/directivity-index.md), [early-reflections](../concepts/early-reflections.md).

## Output & distortion
No compression or max-SPL test found, and no THD at 86/96 dB. Hi-Fi News reports THD of 2% falling to 0.5% across 100 Hz-10 kHz at 90 dB SPL/1 m, higher than average because of low sensitivity ([hifinews-ps-audio-aspen-fr5-lab](../sources/hifinews-ps-audio-aspen-fr5-lab.md)). Stereophile heard the output coarsen into the midrange at high levels with heavy bass (Tier D). Fine for small-to-medium rooms; add a sub for loud listening. See [compression-and-max-spl](../concepts/compression-and-max-spl.md).

## Amplifier load
6 Ω nominal. Impedance magnitude stays above about 5.8 Ω (above the IEC floor of 4.8 Ω), but large phase swings pull EPDR below 4 Ω in 39-46 Hz, 78-139 Hz and 2.8-3.75 kHz (Stereophile) with a minimum near 2.5 Ω. Combined with 81-83 dB sensitivity, use an amplifier comfortable with low impedances; maker suggests 50-150 W. See [impedance-and-epdr](../concepts/impedance-and-epdr.md).

## Critic reviews
- [stereophile-ps-audio-aspen-fr5](../sources/stereophile-ps-audio-aspen-fr5.md) (Stereophile, Kalman Rubinson): hard to fault; excellent treble detail without brightness, generous bass for size, upper midrange slightly depressed vs KEF; sub advised.
- [hifinews-ps-audio-aspen-fr5](../sources/hifinews-ps-audio-aspen-fr5.md) (Hi-Fi News, Mark Craven, 89%): grip, clarity and bass punch beyond its price; stage scale limited.
- [tas-ps-audio-aspen-fr5](../sources/tas-ps-audio-aspen-fr5.md) (The Absolute Sound staff): bass the standout, balanced, slightly warm mids, excellent imaging.

## User reviews
Only 3 owner reports, all from the maker-run forum, so anecdotal ([users-ps-audio-aspen-fr5-2026-10-11](../sources/users-ps-audio-aspen-fr5-2026-10-11.md)): 3 of 3 happy with bass without a sub, 2 of 3 praise soundstage. No QC problems reported.

## Conflicts
- Sensitivity: 81.3 dB (EAC, computed) vs 82.6 dB (Stereophile) vs 81.7-84 dB (HFN) vs 83.5 dB claimed; the maker figure is 2.2 dB above the Tier-A computation.
- Bass: f3 51 Hz (anechoic Klippel) vs -6 dB at 34 Hz (Stereophile nearfield) and 40 Hz (HFN); these are f6-type, nearfield-assisted numbers, and f6 36 Hz computed is consistent.
