## Summary
The Chesky Audio LC1 is a hand-built, direct-sale, 8 Ω two-way with a 3D-printed cabinet, a 6.5" polypropylene woofer, 1" dome tweeter and two 8" side-mounted passive radiators, about $996 per pair. Erin's Klippel data show a listening window of ±2.7 dB with a mid-band dip, f3 near 70 Hz, and a very high impedance that is easy on amplifiers. The pair he measured differed audibly from each other, and vertical lobing below the tweeter axis means it must be heard on or slightly above tweeter axis. Two critics were enthusiastic; the one measurement source was not. Best treated as unproven until a second measurement appears.

## Directivity
Horizontal -6 dB beamwidth about 123°, vertical about 120° (Erin notes wide horizontal dispersion that narrows quickly with frequency). DI is a major discontinuity (ERDI residual 3.7 dB); SPDI rises from 5.2 dB at 1 kHz to 8.6 dB at 10 kHz. Early-reflection dip near 1.5 kHz is 0.9 dB, under the 3 dB flag. Erin reports severe vertical lobing just below tweeter axis, attributed to the first-order crossover, so listen on or slightly above tweeter height. On-axis minus listening window above 4 kHz is +0.8 dB, so aiming straight at the listener is fine; Erin also advises that. See [directivity-index](../concepts/directivity-index.md), [early-reflections](../concepts/early-reflections.md).

## Output & distortion
Distortion at 86 dB is about 1.3% at worst (peak near 1.3 kHz), roughly 0.8% in the low mids; at 96 dB it reaches about 3.5% near 1.3 kHz, 1.5-1.8% across 150-700 Hz (graph-read). Erin saw cone-edge resonance in the multitone test. Compression at 96 dB is about 1.1 dB and at 102 dB reaches about 2-2.5 dB; Erin's summary puts long-term max near 95 dB. Fine for near/mid-field in small rooms; low sensitivity plus limited headroom argue against large rooms or loud levels. See [compression-and-max-spl](../concepts/compression-and-max-spl.md).

## Amplifier load
Nominal 8 Ω, minimum 8.4 Ω above 80 Hz, EPDR 5.4 Ω, phase within roughly ±35°. Impedance peaks about 23 Ω near 100 Hz and 25.7 Ω near 1.8 kHz (graph-read). Easy load, but 81 dB sensitivity means it wants a capable amplifier (maker advises 30 W minimum). See [impedance-and-epdr](../concepts/impedance-and-epdr.md).

## Critic reviews
- [eac-chesky-audio-lc1](../sources/eac-chesky-audio-lc1.md) (Erin's Audio Corner, Tier D notes): found it dull, thin in the vocal range with little bass without wall reinforcement; not recommended without EQ and a sub.
- [futureaudiophile-chesky-audio-lc1](../sources/futureaudiophile-chesky-audio-lc1.md) (Future Audiophile): near-neutral balance, wide soundstage, clean bass for the size; a sub helped on dense tracks.
- [enjoythemusic-chesky-audio-lc1](../sources/enjoythemusic-chesky-audio-lc1.md) (Enjoy the Music): transparent midrange, very wide centre image, usable bass to 40 Hz; best small monitor he knows under $1,000.

## User reviews
No user reports ingested yet; no independent owner reports found ([users-chesky-audio-lc1](../sources/users-chesky-audio-lc1.md)). QC: one measured pair mismatch (Erin); one-person hand production with a 4-6 week quoted lead time.

## Conflicts
- Bass: Enjoy the Music reports usable output to 40 Hz and Future Audiophile calls bass excellent; Erin measures f3 70 Hz (f10 49 Hz) and heard almost no bass. Measurement is Tier A; the reviews are Tier D.
- Tonal balance: Future Audiophile "near neutral" vs Erin's 300-700 Hz dip and ±2.7 dB window; possibly sample variation, since Erin's two samples differed.
- Sensitivity: 81.2 dB measured vs 84 dB on the maker page (and 86 dB cited by Erin, Future Audiophile text and Enjoy the Music); 2.8-4.8 dB shortfall.
- Lead time: 4-6 weeks vs 1-2 weeks on the same maker page.
